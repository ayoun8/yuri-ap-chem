Part B — Free Response

FRQ 1 — Measurement quality

A student determines the density of a metal with an accepted density of 8.96 g/cm³. Three trials give 8.51, 8.50, and 8.52 g/cm³.
(a) Describe the precision of the measurements and justify your answer.
(b) Describe the accuracy and justify your answer.
(c) Calculate the percent error using the mean experimental density.
(d) The balance used was later found to read 0.20 g too low at every mass. Identify this as primarily systematic or random error and explain one likely effect on accuracy/precision.

FRQ 2 — Dimensional analysis + sig figs

A runner travels 7.50 km in 32.4 min.
(a) Convert 7.50 km to meters using a conversion factor.
(b) Convert 32.4 min to seconds.
(c) Calculate the runner's average speed in m/s with correct significant figures.
(d) Explain why defined conversion factors do not reduce the number of significant figures in the final answer.

FRQ 3 — Atomic bookkeeping + nomenclature

Consider the ion ⁵⁶₂₆Fe³⁺ and the compound Fe₂(SO₄)₃.
(a) Determine the numbers of protons, neutrons, and electrons in ⁵⁶Fe³⁺.
(b) Name Fe₂(SO₄)₃.
(c) Show how the charge of the Fe ion can be determined from the formula Fe₂(SO₄)₃.
(d) Explain why the Roman numeral in the name does not represent the number of Fe atoms.

FRQ 4 — Physical vs chemical at the particle level

A sealed container initially contains molecules of a pure substance AB. After heating, a particle diagram shows separate A₂ and B₂ molecules.
(a) Is the process physical or chemical? Justify using particle identity.
(b) Would total mass of the sealed container be expected to change? Explain.
(c) In a different trial, heating only causes the same AB molecules to spread fartthe apart. Classify that process and justify.
(d) Give one macroscopic observation that may suggest a chemical reaction but is not by itself sufficient proof.

FRQ scoring guides

FRQ 1 — Measurement quality — scoring guide

(a) High precision: the trials differ by only 0.02 g/cm³ overall.
(b) Poor accuracy: values are clustered around ≈8.51, noticeably below 8.96.
(c) Mean = 8.51 g/cm³. % error = |8.51−8.96|/8.96×100 = 5.02%.
(d) Systematic error. A consistent bias shifts measurements in the same direction and therefore harms accuracy; repeated values can still remain precise.

FRQ 2 — Dimensional analysis + sig figs — scoring guide

(a) 7.50 km×1000 m/1 km = 7.50×10³ m.
(b) 32.4 min×60 s/1 min = 1.944×10³ s.
(c) 7500/1944 = 3.858... → 3.86 m/s (3 sig figs).
(d) Defined conversions such as 1000 m = 1 km and 60 s = 1 min are exact, so uncertainty comes from measured quantities (7.50 and 32.4), not the conversion definitions.

FRQ 3 — Atomic bookkeeping + nomenclature — scoring guide

(a) p=26; n=56−26=30; e=26−3=23.
(b) iron(III) sulfate.
(c) Each sulfate is 2−; three sulfates total −6. Two Fe must total +6, so each Fe is +3.
(d) Roman numerals state the metal's charge/oxidation state in the compound. Atom count is represented by the formula's subscripts, not the name's Roman numeral.

FRQ 4 — Physical vs chemical at the particle level — scoring guide

(a) Chemical. AB molecules were converted into different molecular species, A₂ and B₂; bonds/particle identities changed.
(b) No. In a sealed system, mass is conserved even though atoms are rearranged.
(c) Physical. The same AB particles remain; only spacing/state changes.
(d) Examples include bubbles, color change, temperature change, or odor. These are clues; classification ultimately depends on formation of new substances.
