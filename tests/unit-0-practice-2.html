Part B — Free Response

FRQ 1 — Accuracy, precision, and percent error

A student measures the density of a solid whose accepted density is 5.32 g/cm³. Three trials give 5.08, 5.09, and 5.07 g/cm³.
(a) Comment on the precision of the measurements and justify.
(b) Comment on the accuracy and justify.
(c) Calculate the percent error using the mean experimental density.
(d) The instrument is later found to produce a constant low reading. Classify this as systematic or random error and explain its likely effect.

FRQ 2 — Dimensional analysis + significant figures

A cyclist travels 12.5 km in 41.2 min.
(a) Convert the distance to meters using dimensional analysis.
(b) Convert the time to seconds.
(c) Calculate average speed in m/s using correct significant figures.
(d) Explain why the conversion factors used in parts (a) and (b) do not limit the final significant figures.

FRQ 3 — Atomic structure + nomenclature

Consider the ion ⁶⁵₂₉Cu²⁺ and the compound Cu(NO₃)₂.
(a) Determine the numbers of protons, neutrons, and electrons in ⁶⁵Cu²⁺.
(b) Name Cu(NO₃)₂.
(c) Show how the copper charge can be determined from the formula.
(d) Explain why parentheses are needed around NO₃ in the formula.

FRQ 4 — Physical vs. chemical change

A sealed container initially contains only molecules XY₂. After a process occurs, the container contains X₂ molecules and Y₂ molecules.
(a) Classify the process as physical or chemical and justify.
(b) Would total mass in the sealed container change? Explain.
(c) In a separate experiment, the same XY₂ molecules simply become farther apart without changing identity. Classify this second process.
(d) Give one observation that might suggest a reaction occurred, and explain why that observation alone is not always conclusive.

FRQ scoring guides

FRQ 1 — Accuracy, precision, and percent error — scoring guide

(a) High precision because the values are very close together.
(b) Poorer accuracy because the measurements cluster around 5.08 g/cm³ rather than 5.32 g/cm³.
(c) Mean = 5.08 g/cm³. % error = |5.08−5.32|/5.32 ×100 = 4.51%.
(d) Systematic error. A constant bias shifts results in one direction, decreasing accuracy while precision can remain high.

FRQ 2 — Dimensional analysis + significant figures — scoring guide

(a) 12.5 km × 1000 m/1 km = 1.25×10⁴ m.
(b) 41.2 min × 60 s/1 min = 2472 s.
(c) 12500/2472 = 5.0566... → 5.06 m/s (3 sig figs).
(d) Defined metric/time conversion factors are exact, so uncertainty comes from the measured values 12.5 and 41.2.

FRQ 3 — Atomic structure + nomenclature — scoring guide

(a) p=29; n=65−29=36; e=29−2=27.
(b) copper(II) nitrate.
(c) Each nitrate is 1−. Two nitrates total −2, so Cu must be +2.
(d) NO₃⁻ is a polyatomic ion that stays together. Two copies are required, so parentheses enclose the ion before applying the subscript 2.

FRQ 4 — Physical vs. chemical change — scoring guide

(a) Chemical because the original XY₂ particles became different molecular species, meaning bonds/particle identities changed.
(b) No. In a sealed system, atoms are rearranged but total mass is conserved.
(c) Physical because particle identity is unchanged; only spacing/phase changed.
(d) Examples: bubbles, temperature change, color change, odor. These can be clues, but some can also occur during physical changes, so formation of new substances is the deciding criterion.
