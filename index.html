Test 1 Priority

Empirical vs Molecular Formula — Complete Guide

Know cold

Empirical formula = simplest whole-number ratio of atoms. Molecular formula = actual number of each atom in one molecule and is always a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula.

Empirical formula

Answers: “What is the simplest ratio?”

C₆H₁₂O₆ → CH₂O

Molecular formula

Answers: “How many atoms are actually in the molecule?”

CH₂O × 6 → C₆H₁₂O₆
Method A: Given masses of each element
Convert every element from grams to moles.
Never compare gram amounts directly. Atomic masses differ.
Divide every mole amount by the smallest mole amount.
This normalizes one element to 1.
Inspect the ratios.
If they are close to whole numbers, use them as subscripts. If not, multiply all ratios by the same small integer.
Write the empirical formula.
Ratio after dividingWhat to do
1.00, 2.00, 3.00…Use directly.
1.50Multiply all ratios by 2.
1.33 or 2.67Multiply all by 3.
1.25 or 1.75Multiply all by 4.
1.20, 1.40, 1.60, 1.80Often multiply all by 5.
Do not round 1.50 to 2. A ratio of 1 : 1.5 becomes 2 : 3, not 1 : 2.
Method B: Given percentages
Assume 100.0 g of compound.
Then each percentage becomes the same number of grams: 40.0% C → 40.0 g C.
Convert grams to moles for every element.
Divide by the smallest mole amount.
Make the ratio whole-numbered and write the empirical formula.
How to get the molecular formula
Find the empirical formula first.
Calculate the empirical-formula mass.
Find the multiplier n.
n = molar mass of compound ÷ empirical-formula mass
Round n to the nearest whole number only if the chemistry clearly supports it.
Multiply every empirical subscript by n.
Summer packet example: Fe and O

Given: 11.66 g Fe, 5.01 g O; molar mass = 320 g/mol.

Fe: 11.66 ÷ 55.845 = 0.2088 mol
O: 5.01 ÷ 15.999 = 0.3131 mol
Divide by 0.2088 → Fe : O = 1.000 : 1.500

Multiply both by 2:

2 : 3 → empirical formula Fe₂O₃
empirical mass ≈ 159.69 g/mol
320 ÷ 159.69 ≈ 2
Packet worksheet method: empirical = Fe₂O₃; molecular-form calculation = Fe₄O₆.
Chemistry convention: ionic compounds are normally written as simplest-ratio formula units, so Fe₂O₃ is the standard chemical formula. The packet is using this item to practice the empirical/molecular calculation procedure.
Summer packet example: Sn and F
Sn: 5.28 ÷ 118.71 = 0.04448 mol
F: 3.37 ÷ 18.998 = 0.1774 mol
Divide by 0.04448 → 1 : 3.99 ≈ 1 : 4
Empirical formula = SnF₄
empirical mass ≈ 194.70 g/mol; 584.1 ÷ 194.70 = 3
Packet worksheet molecular-form calculation = Sn₃F₁₂.
Fast error check
  • If you used grams as subscripts, restart: convert to moles first.
  • If you have decimal subscripts, you are not finished.
  • If your “molecular formula” is not an integer multiple of the empirical formula, something is wrong.
  • If n is nowhere near an integer, recheck molar masses and rounding.
  • Do not simplify the final molecular formula after multiplying; simplification would turn it back into an empirical formula.

11 problems

Filling rules: Aufbau order, Pauli exclusion, and Hund's rule. Across a period, effective nuclear charge generally rises, atomic radius decreases, and electronegativity/ionization energy generally rise.

Complete packet answers

Unit 0 · Unit 0 Quick Reference
Physical vs chemical: physical = composition unchanged; chemical = atoms rearrange into different substances.
Sig figs: ×/÷ → fewest sig figs. +/− → fewest decimal places.
Atomic structure: Z=p; A=p+n; neutral p=e; cation loses e⁻; anion gains e⁻.
Ionic formulas: total positive and negative charge must sum to zero.
Acids: -ate→-ic; -ite→-ous; binary acids use hydro-…-ic.
Molecular compounds: two nonmetals use prefixes; mono- is not used on the first element.
Most dangerous traps: Roman numeral is charge, not atom count; do not use molecular prefixes for ionic compounds; boiling is physical; average atomic mass is not mass number; units must cancel in dimensional analysis.
Topic 0A · Chemistry & Scientific Problem Solving
Topic 0A

Chemistry & Scientific Problem Solving

Conceptual
What chemistry studies

The notes define chemistry as the science dealing with matter and the changes matter undergoes. Matter has mass and occupies space.

Exam translation: chemistry questions often ask what matter is made of, how particles are arranged, what properties matter has, or how one substance changes into another.
Observation → hypothesis → experiment
State the problem and make observations. Quantitative observations involve numbers/measurement; qualitative observations do not.
Form a possible explanation. The notes call this a hypothesis.
Perform experiments to test it. Results can lead to modification and further testing.
StatementType
“The solution is blue.”Qualitative observation
“The mass is 12.41 g.”Quantitative observation
“The color changed because a new product formed.”Hypothesis/explanation
Theory vs law

In the notes, a theory explains while a law states general observed behavior. The law example is conservation of mass: mass remains unchanged through chemical change.

A theory does not become a law simply because it is “proven more.” They answer different kinds of questions.

Particle-level distinction

The notes emphasize that boiling water separates H₂O molecules from one another without splitting individual H₂O molecules. By contrast, electrolysis breaks water molecules apart and rearranges atoms.

Color or energy change can be evidence of a reaction, but the defining criterion is new chemical composition.

Critical skill
Write the given quantity and unit.
Multiply by a conversion factor. Put the unwanted unit on the opposite side so it cancels.
Keep chaining factors until the desired unit is the only one left.
Then calculate.

Accuracy, Precision & Percent Error

Data analysis
Accuracy = closeness to actual/accepted value.
Precision = closeness of repeated measurements to each other.

1.1 · The Mole, Avogadro's Number & Molar Mass

The mole is a counting unit

One mole contains 6.022 × 10²³ elementary entities. Match the entity to the substance: atoms, molecules, formula units, or ions.

1 mol = 6.022 × 10²³ particles

Mass ↔ moles

n = m / M

Moles ↔ particles

N = nNₐ

Atomic mass unit vs molar mass

An isotope's microscopic mass can be stated in amu; one mole has the corresponding numerical mass in grams. The source notes use 1 amu ≈ 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ g.

Do not confuse: mass number is for one isotope and is a whole number; periodic-table atomic mass is a weighted isotopic average.
SubstanceParticleMass term
Molecular compoundmoleculemolar / relative molecular mass
Ionic compoundformula unitmolar / relative formula mass
Worked conversion

23.0 g CO₂ → moles → molecules

M(CO₂)=44.01 g mol⁻¹
23.0 g ÷ 44.01 = 0.523 mol CO₂
0.523 × 6.022×10²³ = 3.15×10²³ molecules
Common mistake: grams do not convert directly to particles; go through moles.
1.2 · Isotopes, Average Atomic Mass & Mass Spectrometry

Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same proton count but different neutron counts.

protons = Z   |   neutrons = A − Z   |   neutral electrons = Z
Same mass number does not mean isotopes. ⁴⁰K and ⁴⁰Ar are different elements; ⁹⁰Sr and ⁹⁴Sr are isotopes.

Weighted average

average atomic mass = Σ(isotope mass × fractional abundance)
Examples
Ne: (22)(0.0801)+(21)(0.0199)+(20)(0.9000)=20.18 amu
85/87 example: (85)(0.71)+(87)(0.29)=85.58 amu

In a mass spectrum, peak position tracks isotope mass information and relative intensity tracks abundance. The weighted average must fall between isotope masses and closer to the more abundant isotope.

1.3 · Empirical & Molecular Formula

Empirical formula

Simplest whole-number atom ratio.

C₆H₆ → CH

Molecular formula

Actual atom counts; whole-number multiple of empirical formula.

CH × 6 → C₆H₆

Percent composition → empirical formula

Assume 100 g.
Convert each mass to moles.
Divide all by the smallest mole amount.
Clear recognizable fractions. ×2 for .500; ×3 for .333/.667; ×4 for .250/.750.
Write the simplest integer ratio.
Class example: 40.1% C, 6.60% H, 53.3% O

1.7 · Periodicity & Coulombic Explanations

Recurring outer-electron configurations create periodic properties. Group members have analogous valence structures and often similar chemistry.

PropertyAcross →Down ↓Reason
Atomic radiusgenerally decreasesincreasesacross: greater nuclear charge; down: more shells/shielding
1st ionization energygenerally increasesdecreaseselectron attraction changes with charge, distance, shielding
Electronegativitygenerally increasesgenerally decreasessmaller/high-Zeff atoms pull bonding electrons more strongly
Electron affinityperiodic with exceptionsgeneral weakening tendencysubshell stability creates anomalies
Strong explanation: cite nuclear charge, shielding, occupied shells, distance, and Coulombic attraction—not merely “because it is farther right.”
Practice examples

Lowest IE among Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs: Cs.

K vs Br radius: K larger.

P vs N IE: P lower because it is in a higher shell and more shielded.

Successive ionization energies

A large jump after removing n electrons indicates n valence electrons were removed before entering a tightly bound core shell.
