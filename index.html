Section IX

Stoichiometry, Step by Step

A beginner-friendly guide to mole conversions, balanced-equation ratios, limiting reactants, gas calculations, and empirical and molecular formulas.

Balance first Convert to moles Use coefficients Convert to final unit

The Big Idea

A balanced chemical equation is a recipe written in moles. Most stoichiometry problems follow the same path:

1. Given amount
2. Moles of given
3. Moles wanted
4. Wanted unit

Conversions to know

moles = grams ÷ molar mass
grams = moles × molar mass
1 mol = 6.022 × 10²³ particles
At STP: 1 mol gas = 22.4 L gas
Most important rule: coefficients in a balanced equation create mole ratios. Subscripts do not.

Before solving

1. Sodium and Lithium Bromide

Problem: 30.5 g sodium reacts with excess lithium bromide. Find the mass of lithium produced.

Chemically, sodium does not normally replace lithium because lithium is higher in the activity series. The worksheet appears designed to practice the math, so the assumed reaction is shown below.
Step 1: Write and balance the assumed reaction.
Na + LiBr → NaBr + Li
The equation is already balanced 1:1:1:1.
Step 2: Convert grams Na to moles Na.
30.5 g Na × (1 mol Na / 22.99 g Na) = 1.33 mol Na
Step 3: Use the mole ratio.
1.33 mol Na × (1 mol Li / 1 mol Na) = 1.33 mol Li
Step 4: Convert moles Li to grams Li.
1.33 mol Li × (6.94 g Li / 1 mol Li) = 9.21 g Li
Worksheet-intended answer: 9.21 g Li

2. Gas Volume to Molecules

Problem: How many molecules are in 100 L of CO₂ at STP?

Step 1: Convert liters to moles.
100 L CO₂ × (1 mol CO₂ / 22.4 L CO₂) = 4.46 mol CO₂
Step 2: Convert moles to molecules.
4.46 mol × (6.022 × 10²³ molecules / 1 mol) = 2.69 × 10²⁴ molecules
2.69 × 10²⁴ molecules CO₂

3. Propane Combustion

Problem: How many grams of propane are needed to produce 45.9 g water?

Step 1: Balance the combustion reaction.
C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O
Step 2: Convert grams H₂O to moles H₂O.
45.9 g H₂O × (1 mol / 18.02 g) = 2.55 mol H₂O
Step 3: Use the mole ratio.
2.55 mol H₂O × (1 mol C₃H₈ / 4 mol H₂O) = 0.637 mol C₃H₈
Step 4: Convert propane moles to grams.
0.637 mol × 44.09 g/mol = 28.1 g C₃H₈
28.1 g C₃H₈

4. Molecules to Moles

Problem: How many moles are in 3.02 × 10²⁶ molecules of water?

Use Avogadro’s number as the conversion factor.
(3.02 × 10²⁶ molecules) × (1 mol / 6.022 × 10²³ molecules) = 5.02 × 10² mol
5.02 × 10² mol H₂O, or 502 mol H₂O

5. Empirical and Molecular Formula: Fe and O

Problem: A compound contains 11.66 g Fe and 5.01 g O. Its molar mass is 320 g/mol.

Step 1: Convert each element to moles.
Fe: 11.66 g ÷ 55.85 g/mol = 0.2088 mol
O: 5.01 g ÷ 16.00 g/mol = 0.3131 mol
Step 2: Divide by the smaller number.
Fe: 0.2088 ÷ 0.2088 = 1.00
O: 0.3131 ÷ 0.2088 = 1.50
Step 3: Make the ratio whole numbers. Multiply both by 2:
Fe₁O₁.₅ × 2 → Fe₂O₃
Step 4: Find the empirical-formula mass.
2(55.85) + 3(16.00) = 159.70 g/mol
Step 5: Find the multiplier.
320 ÷ 159.70 ≈ 2
Empirical formula: Fe₂O₃
Molecular formula by the worksheet method: Fe₄O₆

Note: ionic compounds are normally written in the simplest ratio, so iron(III) oxide is conventionally written Fe₂O₃.

6. Limiting Reactant: Sodium Phosphate and Barium Nitrate

Problem: 3.50 g sodium phosphate reacts with 6.40 g barium nitrate. Find grams of barium phosphate.

Step 1: Write correct formulas and balance.
2Na₃PO₄ + 3Ba(NO₃)₂ → Ba₃(PO₄)₂ + 6NaNO₃
Step 2: Convert each reactant to moles.
Na₃PO₄: 3.50 g ÷ 163.94 g/mol = 0.0213 mol
Ba(NO₃)₂: 6.40 g ÷ 261.35 g/mol = 0.0245 mol
Step 3: Calculate how much product each could make.
From Na₃PO₄: 0.0213 × (1 / 2) = 0.0107 mol product
From Ba(NO₃)₂: 0.0245 × (1 / 3) = 0.00816 mol product
Step 4: Choose the smaller product amount. Barium nitrate makes less product, so it is the limiting reactant.
Step 5: Convert product moles to grams.
0.00816 mol × 601.93 g/mol = 4.91 g
4.91 g Ba₃(PO₄)₂

7. Empirical and Molecular Formula: Sn and F

Step 1: Convert grams to moles.
Sn: 5.28 ÷ 118.71 = 0.0445 mol
F: 3.37 ÷ 19.00 = 0.177 mol
Step 2: Divide by the smaller number.
Sn: 0.0445 ÷ 0.0445 = 1.00
F: 0.177 ÷ 0.0445 ≈ 4.00
Step 3: Write the empirical formula.
SnF₄
Step 4: Find the multiplier.
Empirical mass = 118.71 + 4(19.00) = 194.71 g/mol
584.1 ÷ 194.71 ≈ 3
Empirical formula: SnF₄
Molecular formula by the worksheet method: Sn₃F₁₂

8. Octane Combustion

Step 1: Balance the combustion reaction.
2C₈H₁₈ + 25O₂ → 16CO₂ + 18H₂O
Step 2: Convert octane to moles.
10.0 g ÷ 114.23 g/mol = 0.0875 mol C₈H₁₈
Step 3: Use the mole ratio.
0.0875 × (25 mol O₂ / 2 mol C₈H₁₈) = 1.09 mol O₂
Step 4: Convert O₂ to grams.
1.09 mol × 32.00 g/mol = 35.0 g O₂
35.0 g O₂

9. Sodium Azide Decomposition

Step 1: Balance the decomposition.
2NaN₃ → 2Na + 3N₂
Step 2: Convert N₂ grams to moles.
34.8 g ÷ 28.02 g/mol = 1.24 mol N₂
Step 3: Use the mole ratio.
1.24 mol N₂ × (2 mol NaN₃ / 3 mol N₂) = 0.828 mol NaN₃
Step 4: Convert NaN₃ moles to grams.
0.828 mol × 65.02 g/mol = 53.8 g NaN₃
53.8 g NaN₃

10. Limiting Reactant: Ammonia and Oxygen

Step 1: Balance the equation.
4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O
Step 2: Convert both reactants to moles.
NH₃: 1.50 g ÷ 17.03 g/mol = 0.0881 mol
O₂: 2.75 g ÷ 32.00 g/mol = 0.0859 mol
Step 3: Calculate product from each reactant.
From NH₃: 0.0881 × (4 NO / 4 NH₃) = 0.0881 mol NO
From O₂: 0.0859 × (4 NO / 5 O₂) = 0.0688 mol NO
Step 4: Identify the limiting reactant. Oxygen makes less NO, so oxygen is limiting.
Step 5: Convert NO to grams.
0.0688 mol × 30.01 g/mol = 2.06 g NO
2.06 g NO

Fast test-day checklist
  1. Balance the equation.
  2. Convert the given quantity to moles.
  3. Use a coefficient-based mole ratio.
  4. Convert to the requested final unit.
  5. If both reactants are given, test both for the limiting reactant.
  6. Round only after the final calculation.
