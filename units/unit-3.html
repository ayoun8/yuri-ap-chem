1. Identify intermolecular forces
ForceWhen it appears
London dispersionPresent in all atoms and molecules
Dipole–dipoleBetween polar molecules
Hydrogen bondingWhen H is bonded to N, O, or F
2. Use IMFs to predict boiling point & vapor pressure
Stronger IMFs: generally higher boiling point and lower vapor pressure.

For C₂H₆ vs C₃H₈ vs C₄H₁₀, C₄H₁₀ has the highest boiling point because London dispersion forces get stronger as molecular size increases.

For HCl vs H₂O vs PH₃, H₂O has the highest boiling point because of hydrogen bonding.

3. Ideal gas behavior
Gases behave most ideally at high temperature and low pressure.

Among CO₂, NH₃, and F₂, F₂ behaves most ideally because weaker intermolecular forces favor ideal behavior.

4. Mole fraction & partial pressure
Xᵢ = nᵢ / ntotal
Pᵢ = XᵢPtotal
Convert masses to moles. 28 g N₂ = 1.0 mol; 16 g O₂ = 0.5 mol; 16 g CH₄ = 1.0 mol. Total = 2.5 mol.
X(O₂) = 0.5 / 2.5 = 0.20
P(O₂) = (0.20)(3.00 atm) = 0.600 atm
Mole fraction O₂ = 0.20; partial pressure O₂ = 0.600 atm.
5. Ideal gas law
PV = nRT
Temperature must be Kelvin. 27.0°C ≈ 300 K.
n = (2.00 atm)(10.0 L) ÷ [(0.0821 L·atm·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹)(300 K)]
0.812 mol H₂
6. Identify an unknown gas from molar mass

Use 22.4 L/mol at STP.

44.8 mL = 0.0448 L → 0.0448 L × (1 mol / 22.4 L) = 0.002 mol
molar mass = 0.064 g / 0.002 mol = 32 g/mol
Diatomic gas identity = O₂
7. Molarity & dilution
M = mol / L
Ni²⁺ in 250.0 mL of 0.50 M NiSO₄ 0.250 L × 0.50 mol/L = 0.125 mol NiSO₄ = 0.125 mol Ni²⁺.
M₁V₁ = M₂V₂
Dilution (18 M)(V₁) = (6 M)(500.0 mL), so V₁ ≈ 167 mL.
0.125 mol Ni²⁺; 167 mL of 18 M stock.
8. Water solubility from IMFs

Which is most water soluble: C₃H₈, CH₂O, CH₃OH, or HCl? Answer: CH₃OH.

CH₃OH forms strong, favorable interactions with polar water, including hydrogen bonds.
