4. Limiting reactant & excess reactant
CH₄ + 2 O₂ → CO₂ + 2 H₂O
Convert both reactants to moles. 32 g CH₄ = 2 mol CH₄. 32 g O₂ = 1 mol O₂.
Use the coefficients. 1 mol O₂ can react with only 0.5 mol CH₄, so O₂ runs out first.
Limiting reactant = O₂
1 mol O₂ → 1 mol H₂O → 18 g H₂O
CH₄ left = 2.0 − 0.5 = 1.5 mol → 24 g
18 g H₂O produced; 24 g CH₄ remains.
5. Particle diagrams
2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O

Group particles according to the coefficients: every complete set of 2 H₂ + 1 O₂ becomes 2 H₂O. Anything that cannot fit into another complete stoichiometric set remains as excess reactant.

Do not pair one H₂ with one O₂. The coefficients are the recipe.
6. Titration concentration
HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
Moles NaOH used (0.0300 L)(0.10 mol/L) = 0.00300 mol NaOH.

The reaction is 1:1, so moles HCl = 0.00300 mol.

M(HCl) = 0.00300 mol / 0.0200 L = 0.15 M
