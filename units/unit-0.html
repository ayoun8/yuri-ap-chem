Unit 0 Quick Reference
Physical vs chemical: physical = composition unchanged; chemical = atoms rearrange into different substances.
Sig figs: ×/÷ → fewest sig figs. +/− → fewest decimal places.
Atomic structure: Z=p; A=p+n; neutral p=e; cation loses e⁻; anion gains e⁻.
Ionic formulas: total positive and negative charge must sum to zero.
Acids: -ate→-ic; -ite→-ous; binary acids use hydro-…-ic.
Molecular compounds: two nonmetals use prefixes; mono- is not used on the first element.
Most dangerous traps: Roman numeral is charge, not atom count; do not use molecular prefixes for ionic compounds; boiling is physical; average atomic mass is not mass number; units must cancel in dimensional analysis.
Topic 0A · Chemistry & Scientific Problem Solving
Topic 0A

Chemistry & Scientific Problem Solving

Conceptual
What chemistry studies

The notes define chemistry as the science dealing with matter and the changes matter undergoes. Matter has mass and occupies space.

Exam translation: chemistry questions often ask what matter is made of, how particles are arranged, what properties matter has, or how one substance changes into another.
Observation → hypothesis → experiment
State the problem and make observations. Quantitative observations involve numbers/measurement; qualitative observations do not.
Form a possible explanation. The notes call this a hypothesis.
Perform experiments to test it. Results can lead to modification and further testing.
StatementType
“The solution is blue.”Qualitative observation
“The mass is 12.41 g.”Quantitative observation
“The color changed because a new product formed.”Hypothesis/explanation
Theory vs law

In the notes, a theory explains while a law states general observed behavior. The law example is conservation of mass: mass remains unchanged through chemical change.

A theory does not become a law simply because it is “proven more.” They answer different kinds of questions.

Topic 0A

Physical vs Chemical Properties & Changes

High yield
Property versus change
PhysicalChemical
PropertyColor, odor, density, hardness, solubility, melting point, boiling pointBehavior in reactions such as acid/base reactions or redox
ChangeChemical composition stays the sameAtoms rearrange to produce different composition
Phase-change names
ChangeName
solid→liquidmelting
liquid→gasboiling
gas→liquidcondensing
solid→gassublimation
gas→soliddeposition / reverse sublimation

Every phase change above is physical because identity/composition remains the same.
Particle-level distinction

The notes emphasize that boiling water separates H₂O molecules from one another without splitting individual H₂O molecules. By contrast, electrolysis breaks water molecules apart and rearranges atoms.

Color or energy change can be evidence of a reaction, but the defining criterion is new chemical composition.

Topic 0B · Dimensional Analysis
Topic 0B

Dimensional Analysis

Critical skill
Write the given quantity and unit.
Multiply by a conversion factor. Put the unwanted unit on the opposite side so it cancels.
Keep chaining factors until the desired unit is the only one left.
Then calculate.
9.00 in × (2.54 cm / 1.00 in) = 22.9 cm
If the unit you want to eliminate does not cancel, the factor is upside down.

Kelvin is written K, not °K.

Topic 0B · Accuracy, Precision & Percent Error
Topic 0B

Accuracy, Precision & Percent Error

Data analysis
Accuracy = closeness to actual/accepted value.
Precision = closeness of repeated measurements to each other.
% error = |actual − calculated| / actual × 100
Task 0.5

Actual length = 6.000 m. Set Y measurements are 6.002 and 6.004 m with average 6.003 m.

Most accurate: Set Y. Its average is closest to 6.000 m.
Most precise: Set Y. Its repeated values are also closest to one another.
A set can be precise but inaccurate. “Close together” and “close to true” are separate questions.

Dalton's theory in plain English
  1. Elements consist of atoms.
  2. Dalton originally treated atoms of one element as identical; the notes flag isotopes as a later complication.
  3. Different elements have different atoms.
  4. Compounds contain fixed relative numbers/types of atoms → constant composition.
  5. Chemical reactions rearrange atoms; they are not created/destroyed → conservation of mass.

Topic 0D · Binary Ionic Compounds
Topic 0D

Binary Ionic Compounds

Major target
First decision: metal + nonmetal → ionic. Formula must have net charge zero.
Name → formula algorithm
Write the cation and anion with charges.
Find the smallest whole-number ratio that balances total charge.
Write those ratios as subscripts.
Reduce subscripts if they share a common factor.
Mg²⁺ + N³⁻ → Mg₃N₂ because +6 balances −6
Formula → name algorithm

Name the positive ion first. A monatomic negative ion uses the root + -ide. If the metal has variable charge, infer its charge and show it as a Roman numeral.

CuO: O²⁻ → Cu²⁺ → copper(II) oxide
Cu₂O: total Cu charge = +2, so each Cu⁺ → copper(I) oxide
Roman numeral = charge on one metal ion, not number of metal atoms.

Parentheses rule

If more than one copy of a polyatomic ion is required, put the whole ion in parentheses.

Al³⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → Al₂(SO₄)₃
Al₂SO₄₃ does not correctly preserve three sulfate ions.
Oxoanion oxygen-count pattern
hypo-(element)-ite → (element)-ite → (element)-ate → per-(element)-ate

More oxygen as you move right. When only two are emphasized, -ite has fewer O than -ate: sulfite SO₃²⁻ vs sulfate SO₄²⁻.

The notes also state that thio- indicates sulfur replacing an oxygen atom in an anion.

Topic 0D · Hydrates
Topic 0D

Hydrates

Naming

Hydrates are ionic formula units with water molecules incorporated into the solid structure. Heating can drive off the water; the remaining salt is anhydrous.

CuSO₄·5H₂O → copper(II) sulfate pentahydrate
The number after the dot gives water molecules per ionic formula unit.
